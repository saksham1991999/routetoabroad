**Short answer: you cannot apply for a Chinese student visa without your Admission Notice and JW202 form from the university. Once you have those, the visa itself is straightforward — the real deadline pressure is converting the X1 into a residence permit within 30 days of arrival.**

## The document chain

Everything hangs off three documents, in this order:

1. **Admission Notice** — the university's formal offer, stating programme and dates.
2. **JW202 form** (or JW201 for scholarship students via government channels) — the visa application for study, generated by the provincial education department for the university.
3. **X1 visa** — stamped at your local Chinese embassy/consulate using documents 1 and 2, your passport, application form, photo, and physical examination record.

No JW202, no visa. This is why universities that are slow to issue JW202s wreck September intakes — chase it, or have us chase it.

## Step-by-step: from offer to visa

1. **Receive and check your Admission Notice + JW202** — names, passport number, dates must match your passport exactly. Any mismatch means a re-issue.
2. **Complete the physical examination** on the official form at an approved clinic. Everything must be filled, stamped, with a photo affixed and sealed. Incomplete forms are the #1 avoidable rejection.
3. **Book the embassy/consulate appointment** (or CVASC centre, depending on jurisdiction) and prepare: passport, forms, photo, admission documents, financial proof if asked.
4. **Attend and pay the visa fee** — processing is typically 4–7 working days, with express options at higher cost.
5. **Enter China** — the X1 is single-entry; do not leave China before converting it.
6. **Convert to a Residence Permit within 30 days** at the local Exit-Entry Administration: usually another physical, the university's registration paperwork, and the permit fee. The university international office typically coordinates this in registration week.

## X2 (short courses)

Under 180 days — summer schools, one-semester exchanges. Simpler: no residence permit, but the visa is fixed-term and generally non-extendable. Plan travel outside China after the course, not during.

## Pitfalls that actually cause trouble

- **Name/passport mismatches** between Admission Notice and JW202
- **Physical exams older than 6 months** or missing the seal
- **Entering China on the wrong visa** (L visa "to sort it out later" — no)
- **Departing China before the residence permit is issued** — the X1 dies on exit
- **Booking one-way flights before the visa is granted** — airlines check
