**Short answer: the CSC scholarship can fund your entire Chinese degree, but full awards are competitive and partial awards are the norm — apply broadly, file perfectly, and never pay anyone who promises a guaranteed CSC.**

The China Scholarship Council administers the Chinese Government Scholarship. For students who prepare properly it is the single biggest cost lever in a Chinese degree. For students who prepare badly it is a February-to-April heartbreak. This guide exists to put you in the first group.

## Type A vs Type B — pick your lane

**Type A** goes through your home government: an embassy or ministry nominates candidates. Quotas are small and often bounded by bilateral agreements; check your country's designated receiving agency early.

**Type B** goes direct to Chinese universities that are CSC-designated. Most self-applied students use Type B: you submit to the university and, if they want you, they forward your file with a university recommendation.

## What the money actually looks like

| Component | Full award | Typical partial |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tuition | Covered 100% | 30–100% waiver |
| Dorm | Free on-campus room | Discounted or self-paid |
| Stipend | ~¥2,500–3,500/month | Usually none |
| Medical insurance | Covered | Sometimes covered |

Monthly stipend figures are indicative; the schedule is updated periodically — check the current stipend table on campuschina.org for your intake year.

## The realistic odds

Full CSC awards at top-tier universities (Tsinghua, Peking, Fudan) are highly competitive — think single-digit acceptance for international applicants across all countries. Provincial universities and less-exported programmes have meaningfully better odds. A smart strategy pairs one reach, one target, one safety — exactly like university applications.

## Documents that decide it

1. CSC online application form (generated from the portal)
2. Notarised highest-degree certificate and transcripts
3. Study plan (bachelor's) or research proposal (master's/PhD) — 800+ words, specific
4. Two recommendation letters on letterhead
5. Physical examination form on the official template
6. Passport copy (12+ months validity)
7. Language proof: English-medium certificate, IELTS/TOEFL, or HSK for Chinese-taught tracks
8. Police clearance (postgraduate)
9. Acceptance letter from a supervisor (research degrees — email professors early)

## Timeline that keeps you eligible

- **October–December:** shortlist universities and professors; draft study plan
- **January:** CSC portal opens; notarise documents
- **February–mid-April:** submit — deadlines vary per university, never assume April is safe
- **May–July:** results; pair the award with your Admission Notice and the route-appropriate JW form — **JW202** covers Type B university-route awards and self-funded students, while **JW201** belongs to the Type A government channel. Use whichever form your university issues; either supports the X1 visa application.
- **July–August:** X1 visa, arrival, residence permit
