**Short answer: tourism = L, business meetings = M, short academic exchange = F, degree study = X1, employment = Z. Working or studying on the wrong visa type causes real legal trouble in China — match the visa to the activity.**

China's visa alphabet is dense but logical. These are the categories that matter for India–East Asia travel:

| Category | Purpose | Typical grant | Key requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **L** | Tourism, family visits | 30–60 days/entry | Itinerary + bookings |
| **M** | Business, trade fairs | 30–90 days/entry | Invitation letter from Chinese partner |
| **F** | Exchange, study tours, short visits | Varies | Invitation from Chinese organisation |
| **X1** | Study > 180 days | Duration of programme | Admission Notice + JW202 |
| **X2** | Study ≤ 180 days | Fixed, ≤ 180 days | Admission Notice + JW202 |
| **Z** | Employment | Permit-bound | Work permit + notification |
| **G** | Transit | 1–15 days | Onward ticket |

## L — the tourist visa

Apply with passport, form, photo, a simple day-wise itinerary, hotel bookings and confirmed onward travel. Indian applicants apply through CVASC centres; standard processing runs about 4 working days plus queue time. Multi-entry versions exist for frequent travellers.

## M — the business visa

For sourcing trips, Canton Fair, factory audits and contract negotiations. The invitation letter from the Chinese counterparty is the hinge document — we issue these for trade clients through the Guangzhou office. Employment is explicitly prohibited on M.

## F — exchange and short programmes

Cultural exchanges, study tours and short academic visits. Issued against an invitation from a recognised Chinese organisation. If your "short course" awards credits toward a degree, check with us whether X2 is the safer category.

## X1 / X2 — students

Covered in depth in our [student visa guide](/guides/china-student-visa). Remember: X1 holders must convert to a residence permit within 30 days of arrival, and the X1 dies the moment you exit China before conversion.

## Visa-free transit

China has repeatedly expanded visa-free transit and visa-free entry schemes for short stays in specific provinces and for specific nationalities. These rules change frequently — treat any blog (including older ones) as outdated until you verify against the current policy for your passport and entry port.

## The two classic mistakes

1. **Activity–visa mismatch**: sneaking supplier visits onto an L visa risks fines and exit bans.
2. **Last-minute filing**: golden-week and fair-season queues at CVASC double processing times.
