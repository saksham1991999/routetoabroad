**Short answer: treat sourcing as a verification pipeline, not a shopping trip — audit the factory, inspect at 20%/80% of production, structure payments 30/70, and get the India compliance stack (HS code, BIS/WPC) ready before goods ship. Do that and China sourcing is a superpower; skip it and it's a donation.**

## Step 1: Build the candidate list properly

Alibaba and Canton Fair contacts are candidates, not suppliers. Filter by: manufacturer vs trading company (license scope), product-category match, export history to India, certifications claimed. Aim for 5–8 candidates, then narrow to 2–3 for sampling.

## Step 2: Audit before you negotiate

A one-day factory audit answers questions no certificate can: does the registered address match the plant, what's the real capacity, whose machines are those, how do they handle QC today. Cost is trivial against a bad container. Our Guangzhou desk runs audits with photo-video reports, typically turned around within 72 hours.

## Step 3: Sample like it's a contract (it is)

Golden samples should be dated, signed, sealed in triplicate — factory, you, inspector. Every later dispute resolves against that sample, plus a bilingual spec sheet: materials, tolerances, colours (Pantone), packaging, markings, test standards.

## Step 4: Production with gates

- **~20% checkpoint:** line setup correct? materials as specified? first-piece approvals?
- **~80% checkpoint:** full AQL sampling (Acceptable Quality Limit) against the spec
- **Pre-shipment:** container loading supervision — right goods, right count, right seal

Each gate produces a report before further money moves.

## Step 5: The money structure

| Milestone | Payment | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order confirmation | 30% deposit | Covers materials; never 100% |
| Pre-shipment inspection pass | balance prep | Evidence in hand |
| Against B/L copy | 70% balance | Goods provably on the water |

LC at sight suits established, larger orders. Escrow platforms suit first engagements. Pay universities and suppliers only into corporate accounts — never personal ones.

## Step 6: India compliance before the ship sails

1. **HS classification** — determines duty, restrictions, licensing
2. **BIS (CRS)** for notified electronics; **WPC** for anything wireless
3. **CDSCO** for medical devices, **FSSAI** for food-contact goods
4. **Customs valuation & documentation** — invoice, packing list, B/L, COO, test reports

Paperwork built during production clears in days; paperwork built after arrival costs demurrage.

## Step 7: Logistics choice

- **LCL** (shared container) for trials up to ~12–15 CBM
- **FCL** 20ft/40ft at scale; sea 18–25 days to Indian west coast ports
- **Air** 3–7 days at 5–8× cost — for launches, spares, high-value density goods
