Beijing has been the political and cultural heart of China for over 700 years. Its historical sites are not merely tourist attractions — they are living documents of a civilisation that shaped the entire region. If you are visiting for the first time, these five are non-negotiable.

## 1. The Forbidden City (故宫)

The world's largest palace complex, home to 24 emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties, spans 180 acres and some 980 buildings. Arrive early to beat the crowds and give it at least half a day. Tickets are released online in advance batches and sell out fast — plan weeks ahead, especially around Golden Week.

## 2. The Great Wall at Mutianyu

Badaling is the famous section; Mutianyu is the better one — restored watchtowers, fewer crowds, and a toboggan ride back down. It sits about 70 km from the city, so factor in travel time. Our group tours include private coach transfers and an early start.

## 3. The Temple of Heaven (天坛)

Where emperors performed annual rites for good harvests, this Ming-era complex is a masterpiece of cosmological design. Come in the morning: the surrounding park fills with retirees doing tai chi, chess, and traditional music — a cultural spectacle in its own right.

## 4. The Summer Palace (颐和园)

A UNESCO World Heritage Site built around Kunming Lake. A morning boat ride followed by a walk along the 728-metre Long Corridor is among the most memorable experiences Beijing offers — do it before the tour buses arrive around ten.

## 5. 798 Art District

Not ancient — but indispensable. Cold-war-era Bauhaus factory buildings now house galleries, studios, design boutiques, and some of the city's best coffee. It is the perfect counterpoint to the imperial sites and proof that Beijing's cultural ambition didn't stop in 1420.
