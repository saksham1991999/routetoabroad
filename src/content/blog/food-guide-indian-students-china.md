For most Indian students arriving in China, food is the first and biggest culture shock. The smells are different, the flavours are unfamiliar, and you cannot read the menu. Here is what actually helps — from a student who has been there.

## The international student canteen is your first stop

Most major Chinese universities have an international student cafeteria (留学生食堂) that serves halal options, rice dishes, and occasionally Indian-inspired food. Find yours in the first week and get comfortable with the payment apps — WeChat Pay or Alipay, though your student card often works on campus.

## Indian grocery shops exist — know where they are

Cities like Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Wuhan have Indian grocery stores, often run by South Asian expat families. They stock masala, dal, atta, and sometimes fresh vegetables. The fastest way to find them: ask in the WeChat groups for Indian students in your city.

## Learn these five phrases for restaurants

- 不辣 (Bù là) — not spicy
- 素食 (Sùshí) — vegetarian
- 不含猪肉 (Bù hán zhūròu) — no pork
- 清真 (Qīngzhēn) — halal
- 这个 (Zhège) — this one (point at the menu)

## Chinese dishes Indian palates tend to love

- Mapo tofu (麻婆豆腐) — spicy tofu with a familiar heat profile
- Kung pao chicken (宫保鸡丁) — mildly spiced with peanuts
- Egg fried rice (蛋炒饭) — the safe, satisfying staple
- Hot pot (火锅) — fully customisable and a social event in itself
- Lanzhou beef noodles (兰州拉面) — rich, hearty, and usually halal-friendly

Food adjustment takes about 3–4 weeks; after that most students find a happy routine. And when homesickness hits hard, every major city has Indian restaurants that will feel like home.
